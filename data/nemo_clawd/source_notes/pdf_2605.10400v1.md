## Extracted Abstract Or Lead

We ask whether a perpetual-futures engine could have survived observed conditions on arXiv:2605.10400v1 [q-fin.TR] 11 May 2026 Polymarket’s binary-event prediction markets, and what its design would need to look like if so. We do not propose deployment; the contribution is a specification of a resolution-aware risk-engine framework, an empirical evaluation of that framework as specified, and the design tensions the evaluation reveals. Binary event-contract underlyings have three structural properties absent from cryptoperpetual design assumptions: bounded support with terminal collapse, structurally asymmetric depth near the boundaries, and oracle-mediated discrete resolution. Two non-portability propositions follow: standard basis-only funding paired with continuousvolatility static margin fails to constrain behavior near the boundaries (Proposition 2) and provides insufficient collateral to absorb the bounded-event terminal jump (Proposition 1). The propositions rest on the three structural properties only. We specify a four-component framework: a composite robust index; a jump-aware tiered margin schedule; boundary-aware funding correction; and a multi-stage resolution-zone protocol with leverage compression. Every parameter is either estimated from observable market features with a documented estimator (Section C) or designated a policy parameter and varied in sensitivity analysis (Section E). Estimators and falsifiability thresholds are pre-registered and locked before the empirical evaluation runs. Empirical evaluation uses Polymarket’s PMXT v2 archive for the calendar week 2026- 04-21 to 2026-04-27 (13.
